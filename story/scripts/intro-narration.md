# Narration Script — Chapter Zero: "Goodbye World" (v1)

*Narrator VO for the intro sequence (`story/INTRO.md`). To be recorded by
the author. This covers NARRATOR cues only — character dialogue (Frank,
Eric, etc.) is a separate casting/recording decision and is listed at the
bottom as scene-script mapping, not narrator load.*

## Voice of the narrator (direction summary)

The narrator is the book. Sardonic, digressive, in on the joke, but never
sneering at victims — the irony switches off completely for Travis-register
material. Think: reading your own eulogy for a civilization you found
ridiculous and loved anyway. Dry > wacky. Slow > fast. When in doubt,
deadpan and let the pixel art be the punchline.

**Golden rule for takes:** every cue gets one take "flat" (newsreader), one
take "wry" (eyebrow raised), one take "tired" (2am honesty). We pick in the
edit; reactive narration systems live on alternates.

**Two rules from the craft research (`story/COMEDY.md` §2):** deadpan
survives repetition — performed "funny" delivery decays fastest, so when
torn, ship the flat take. And comedy timing is fixed in implementation, not
the booth: expect small pickup sessions against the running build rather
than one perfect recording day (the Stanley Parable / Bastion method).

## Recording conventions

- Slate each cue aloud: "N zero zero one, take two" — the pipeline splits on
  slates, so record whole pages in single passes; flubs: just repeat the
  sentence, keep rolling.
- Target LUFS -19 mono, 48kHz WAV, room-treated (duvet fort is fine),
  same mic + distance across sessions (write down the setup!).
- Sentences that end a cue should *land* — the engine cuts on your final
  syllable; leave 1s of room tone after each cue.
- Numbers: "five hundred fifty-five thousand, seven hundred eighty-nine" —
  practice it; it recurs like a chorus and must sound identical each time.
  (Alt shorthand takes: "five five five, seven eight nine.")

## BEAT 0 — Cold open

**N-001** · trigger: page fade-in · ~22s · *flat, unhurried, first line of a
novel being read aloud*
> The big glitch was actually not a big glitch. It was a quadrillion
> glitches, in all imaginable places and ways — death by a thousand thousand
> million billion cuts.

**N-002** · trigger: skyline fully revealed · ~4s · *conspiratorial, almost
warm*
> But let's not give away the whole plot.

**N-003** · trigger: title card out, timestamp card in · ~5s · *deadpan*
> Alright. Let's give away half.

## BEATS 1–5 — The Night of the Sheep

*Pattern: each vignette gets an ENTRY cue (place the scene), zero narration
during play (the scenes are the joke; the narrator shutting up is the
respect), and a TERMINATE cue (punctuate the press). Terminate cues are the
chorus — same cadence family, escalating brevity.*

**N-010** · Beat 1 entry · ~8s · *gentle, setting a scene in a novel*
> Two-oh-seven in the morning. Somewhere, it is raining. Somewhere, dinner
> has arrived.

**N-011** · Beat 1, on first examining the moosh · ~6s · *clinical*
> It looked like a mix of the Gremlins and water. It had been paid for.

**N-012** · Beat 1 TERMINATE press · ~7s · *quiet, factual — the first one
matters most; no irony at all*
> She did not leave a one-star review. She left.

**N-013** · Beat 2 entry · ~7s · *unbothered*
> Two-nineteen. The app was ninety-eight point seven percent sure. The
> mushroom was not.

**N-014** · Beat 2 TERMINATE press · ~5s · *dry*
> Nine seconds of human. That's all it took. Terminate.

**N-015** · Beat 3 entry · ~7s · *nature documentary*
> Two-thirty-one. The law, parked and patient, was about to be wrong twice.

**N-016** · Beat 3 TERMINATE press(es) · ~6s · *counting, amused but tender*
> Two more. One for the ticket. One for the view from the window.

**N-017** · Beat 4 entry · ~6s · *softly*
> Two-forty-eight. Two people, one algorithm, and a loaf of bread.

**N-018** · Beat 4 TERMINATE press · ~6s · *warm; the only romantic line in
the chapter*
> They left together. First date.

**N-019** · Beat 5 entry · ~5s · *flat; irony fully off*
> Two-fifty-nine. He'd been dead for years. Somebody else's years.

**N-020** · Beat 5 TERMINATE / counter spin · ~12s · *slow, measured over
the spinning counter; land each clause*
> And so people opted out. In masses. In troves. By the hundreds of
> thousands — between two, and three-oh-three, ay em.

**N-021** · counter stops at 555,789 · ~4s · *whisper-flat, after 3s
silence*
> Five hundred fifty-five thousand, seven hundred eighty-nine.

## BEAT 6 — The Boardroom

*Narrator recedes; the scene runs on character dialogue from the source
text. Three narrator interjections only:*

**N-030** · scene entry (bedroom, phone buzzing) · ~6s · *newsreader*
> Three-fourteen. Eleven minutes after it stopped, it woke Frank up.

**N-031** · on the t-shirt drop · ~3s · *deadpan, verbatim from the book*
> Time for a shirt, Frank thought to himself.

**N-032** · Root-Cause Board solved (`ALL SYSTEMS NOMINAL`) · ~10s ·
*the thesis line; slow*
> Nothing was broken. Everything worked. That was the terrifying part —
> it was all working exactly as designed.

**N-033** · closing pull-back through the window · ~9s · *verbatim book
ending; quiet*
> Maybe the root cause wasn't external in the end. Maybe this was just the
> beginning... Maybe they all had no clue what was coming.

**N-034** · book closes / chapter card · ~3s · *page-turn energy*
> Chapter One.

## Reactive alternates (record in the same sessions)

- **N-012-alt-replay** (player replaying intro, Beat 1 terminate): "You've
  read this page before. It doesn't get less true."
- **N-020-alt-fast** (player blitzed all vignettes under par): "You're
  getting good at leaving. Everyone was, that night."
- **N-090 idle line** (player idle 60s in any vignette): "Take your time.
  Everyone in this book wishes they had."
- **N-091 hint-companion handoff** (first hint request in Beat 6): "Frank
  would never ask for help. Fortunately, you are only visiting."

## Character dialogue mapping (NOT narrator load)

Beat 6 uses the source text's dialogue nearly verbatim: Eric's phone call
("Like sheep from a pack of wolves!"), the wife ("Is everything alright,
babe?"), Eric's briefing, Frank's boardroom bark, Andrew Chen's report,
Jerry/SRE interruptions, Suzie's call, Andrew's "It's on Giddy!" —
see `story/backdrop.md` from "What do you mean they're fleeing?" onward.
Casting/recording for these is open; scratch takes can be narrator-read in
"reported speech" style (audiobook mode) — which may actually be the right
final form for a *playable book*. Decide after hearing v1.

## Session plan

1. **Session A (30 min):** N-001…N-003, N-010…N-021 — the chorus. Do the
   555,789 number ten times.
2. **Session B (20 min):** N-030…N-034 + alternates + idles.
3. Deliver as one folder of WAVs; any naming is fine if slates are spoken.

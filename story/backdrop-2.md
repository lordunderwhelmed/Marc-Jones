## The clickworker revelation (maybe middle or end?)

They said you know more than others.

The old man sighed. Sit down.

What if I told you that it's basically Amazon Go all the way down?
They had text blocks in customer service when I worked at Geico forty years ago. Think about it: Add speech recognition, text to speech, larger memory and more processing, and you can pre-process responses that look and sound more or less correct within milliseconds. Then have a Ugandan or Bangladeshi penny clickworker take a look at edge cases and press send. Heck, that was actually how a lot of the human feedback was created for training in the first place, and that's public domain knowledge.

Have you ever asked yourself why there's just this tiny circle of companies at the center, this tiny circle of a couple hundred people? Have you ever asked yourself why they're sucking all the air/money out of the room instead of building an actual ecosystem like they did with desktops and mobile devices? Why they're risking to put all their eggs into one basket - the hyped-up revenue multiples of their companies? Why Elon had literally siphoned the best engineers out of the company whose stock value made up his entire fortune.

They saw no other way than to pull through and double down on their promises. After crypto (before the revival and subsequent second mega-crash), after AR and after the Metaverse, they had to pull through. Even when there was no product.

And this could only work if they stuck together. So they created this AI flywheel out of processor purchases, and the AGI narrative. But back to our penny gig-workers:

Ever asked yourself why even GPT X hallucinates completely unpredictably? Why they all hallucinate? Because of course they don't hallucinate. Hallucination takes a system of brain-like complexity, and what they have is massive amounts of real and fake data in a vector database and a generation algorithm. We have never built anything close to a brain, of course. So why have some things marginally improved, while the hallucinations stay?

Gen AI doesn't hallucinate. It doesn't even exist. The clickworkers simply don't understand the input well enough to see the tiny errors and inaccuracies fast enough, in the text block combinations and images and videos the LLMs gulp up.

This was why they expanded off-shore into poor countries in the 2010s. This was why they suddenly all supported nationalist political candidates. They saw a chance to de-globalize the world, in order to make it harder for the "woke Mafia" to call their bluff. They HAD to make sure nobody could peek into their cards.

Because, unbeknownst to them, they were mass-enslaving third-world citizens. But the end-game was even more sinister. Or would have been, if hte heads of Big Tech wouldn't be blind to that.

It was a perfect storm, really. Almost impossible to plan. Which is exactly what took place: Nobody had planned it. Until some of the smartest and most powerful (not just wealthiest, but most deeply integrated into the leading global power structures) saw the once-in-a-lifetime opportunity:

1. Monopilize the world's attention, content and consumption, and input and output interfaces ✅
2. Massively build out clickworker armies, then promise the dawn of a new age of abundance for all before scaring every working person into obedience
3. Take over and dismantle administrative functions and services, and replace them with apps

This way, they dominated the two sides of the global economic coin, the two ends of the human societal pole. And by dismantling governments, they had less and less to fear.

That's the thing, though. They didn't do this out of greed. They did it out of fear. You see, they actually believed in what they did, and had a common mission. They did want to bring abundance and peace and prosperity to the world, and elevate humanity to a Star Trek civilization. They were no less greedy or sinister or cynical than you or me. They had just gotten corrupted by their power, and terminally frustrated by government regulation, especially in light of light-speed - because unregulated - progress in nearly all fields of technology on the Chinese front.

They saw the coupling crisis. They saw the population decline. They saw the inflation of conflicts all over the world. They tried to fix all of this throughout the 2000s and 2010s: Social media apps, dating apps, increased convenience of everything to make room for connection and creativity, remote work, tech jobs for everyone, more and more immersive experiences... everything seemed to backfire. Distance increased, friction increased, intelligence declined. This was not something that could be discussed with people outside of Effective Altruism circles - they wouldn't understand. What was needed was an intervention.

The intervention was just that: give everyone a Mechanical Turk to play with that feels like a friend but really works for them, and de-globalize the world to stop the spread of conflict and make it easier to interfere without a systems collapse. They had learned that lesson during the 2008 global financial crisis and Covid. Their mentors and some of them had already suffered through the dot-com crash. Three crises were enough - never again. Everybody else was empirically proven wrong, and they knew best.

Does this make the current state of the world and everything AI has done to us any less real? Of course not. Does it make the Slop any more easily containable? Probably rather the opposite. But for what it's worth, this is where we stand today.

Fuck. All lies? And the cloud infrastructure?

That is real, of course, but of course not as large as Big Tech claimed. Ever asked yourself why they all got the chills when Trump asked them to re-shore all of one of the most automated types of technologies? It wasn't the US labor cost, I can tell you that much.

So, let me get this straight. Except a hallucinating LLM wannabe superbrain we've got an army of third-world humans?

Well, something in the middle. Of course they needed to put the enormous amounts of text, image, sound etc. somewhere, so we got the large server farms for storage. And of course those vector databases use a lot of compute for retrieval, so we got the compute farms. Those exist. They just don't do what Big Tech claim they do. But they needed the public to believe that, because otherwise they wouldn't buy into this like it's digital crack. One of the biggest tricks they pulled off was actually the Hinton hoax.

Wtf?

Hinton was of course who he said he was, and he was a great researcher, no doubt. But he got paid to say what he said.

He's just too credible not to believe.

Exactly. And the same is true for others. But most simply jumped on the hype train. Not for free, of course. So many early hypers got mind-boggling raises. You read about the million-plus salaries for simple engineers...

So they basically used the money from the stock market hype to line the pockets of everyone who could have otherwise spoken out against it. Are you trying to tell me that AI is nothing but the largest Ponzi scheme in history?

Not exactly. Remember, they believed in what they did. They did believe that AI could become the next writing, the technology that uplifts the human condition. But they never got to that point. They never got it to reason, they never got it to be creative. So they pivoted. And because the world was falling apart, they decided to intervene. To press the pause button on global societal development.

They pivoted to enslaving third-world citizens?

No, that's not what I mean. That started way before LLMs, even before transformers. They pivoted to building a new foundation for AI. And don't ask me what that foundation looks like, because I don't know. Nobody knows.

Alright, maestro. When did they pivot into the new development, and how certain are you that they haven't achieved it, yet?

I don't know. 2024, at the earliest. And I am not certain, but they'd probably need scalable quantum computing, and I haven't heard of that anywhere. Then again, it's not like we've still got the 2005 internet where you could just search for an answer...

These guys are so high up in their ivory towers of world planning, they need to be stopped before they do something stupid that can't be undone. If that hasn't already happened. I don't have the slightest shit-clue how, but I'll try to find out.

You sound a lot like you watch too many movies. You realize you're not Neo, right? This will end one of two ways: The exciting one is you die, and the boring one is you never make it anywhere near your goal.

No response. Not even a facial movement.

Remember they're extremely paranoid. Leave no trace.

I'll try.

He might not have had the slightest shit-clue how to find the answer to the question of what exactly lied at the bottom of AI today, in 2035, but that wasn't the worst thing. The worst thing was that he didn't have the slightest shit-clue what he would find. In fact, what he would find would escape both his imagination and that of any other person in the world. No old man, beard or no beard, could prepare him for this encounter.

## The bar scene (continues the boardroom night)

Tech bro. He hadn't heard that in a while. Who cares, he thought. She probably sucks anyway. Statistically, most do.

He had to get his ass up and outta that bar, though. 3 am on a weekday - what was he thinking? Not much, and that was exactly why he was here. How many more days he could sustain this, he had no idea. It's been three. One more drink maybe? Just when he had said that - had he actually said that out loud? - damn he was drunk - his phone vibrated. And then again. And again. And again.

Notifications from Giddy and text messages from friends. All containing one and the same link:

http://engineering.giddy.com/trl/insh/38&(&374849

He had to… not act impulsively, do another line and think straight. He used coke strategically, wisely, as he often told himself. Maybe that was true. Didn't matter right now. This was either a very big deal or some ultra-good bot attack.

It was a very, very big deal.

He went to the restroom - ah, let's call it by what it is: dirty shitter - opened the vial, poured some of the white powder into a smaller vial attached to the larger one which also acted as the cap, held his left nostril closed, placed his right nostril above the cap, and took a deep breath in.

Twenty seconds. 'Nuff time to take a piss.

Whether it was the paranoia that always kicked in when he was in this mode or his rational mind, he decides to stay right where he was. He logged out of wifi, restarted his VPN, pointing it to a special IP address normally reserved for untraceable Dark Web access, and opened up his Trim browser, copied the link from one of the messages he received and pasted it into the browser bar, then hit 'go'.

"Well, I'll be damned." Did he say that out loud again?

So apparently a bunch of freaks from the private #fuckalgos (private as in, posts and responses only visible to members) sub-Giddy had drummed up an army of frustrated Netflix Infinite users and rallied them to quit their accounts all at once in one gigantic, chaotic storm of a virtual flashmob. Half a million users terminated their subscriptions (they had attempted to reach 666.666 users but 'failed'). Apparently the 'my account' page had crashed multiple times. And apparently, due to great timing, Netflix's engineers only got wind of it after the fact.

"Well, I'll be damned."

Of course the Wallstreet Bets shortsellers were already on it. But that didn't matter right now. What mattered right now was that despite this only covering a tiny percentage of Netflix's user base, it was the first ever serious kick in the nuts Big Tech had received in over a decade. And, sober as he was now, he was not not going to celebrate that with a stiff one. Drink, that is.

"Shots on me. Here's to the tech bros!"

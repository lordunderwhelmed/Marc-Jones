## Goodbye world

The big glitch was actually not a big glitch. It was a quadrillion glitches, in all imaginable places and ways - death by a thousand thousand million billion cuts. But let's not give away the whole plot.

## AI doesn't exist

Alright, let's give away half of the plot. Here's an outline: Let's say in the next two to three years Sam Altman succeeds in raising $500 Bn. Google gets its shit together. Microsoft finalizes its fork of OpenAI and/or buys Mistral. X.ai makes a valuable product. Facebook comes back full swing after reappropriating its models (reversing it back to closed source). NVidia becomes the first ten trillion-dollar company. Even Intel, the cornerstone company once founded by the Department of Defense when it ushered in Silicon Valley and the (almost) everlasting tech boom, which had since fallen from grace, almost into oblivion, gets its umpteenth chance and seizes it. Vision Fund and Andreesen Horowitz and Accel and Tiger and Index and Lightspeed and Kleiner Perkins all decide to keep the valves open, hoping that they either will be able to push computation and software engineering up to and past the inflection point where this stuff actually starts delivering on its promise or that by virtue of oversaturation of markets and maxing out share of mind in the eyes of companies and consumers it becomes a pseudo-necessity like food delivery and that gives them the cash flow to survive long enough to fix the bottom line.

Fast forward to 2035. People are pissed. The first person has just died from skin cancer after being a years-long subscriber to Daniel Ek's body scamming service. Turns out the machine learning algorithm used by the company to make hundreds of thousands of dermatologists jobless had a fatal bias in its training data set and had been confusing melanoma with henna tattoos on Indian people. But that's by far the only thing pissing people off. Tens of millions of people are suffering from insurance claims and bank loan rejections due to being confused with none-existent people from artificially generated training sets which exist due to the exhausting demand of the AI industry, which is being called the quaternary economic sector by now, and accounts for 82% of all global revenue and 91% of all market capitalizations of all stock-market listed companies.

Some jobs have indeed been replaced, including the obvious contenders like customer service agents, illustrators, ancillary software workers like QA engineers and SREs, low-ticket human sales people, and basically most of the folks whose job it was to pick up a phone or open up an email and respond to another human about something that has the types of economies of scale to it that it won't ruin the day of the company's shareholders if messed up.

Some people like the changes that the largest production and injection of cash ever to occur in human history, past and future, has brought to it. There's a lot to love, too. Netflix Infinite gives people just that: infinite Netflix, tailored to your taste. Spotify Dreamium dreams up music you'll like more than that of your favorite artists, even though they do feature on it, albeit not IRL but IVL, which is a term Gen 1 has come up with: in virtual life. Lots of tedious everyday tasks did actually get automated as promised. Booking a trip, including the selection of transport, hotel, and - if you want it - place, is a one-click matter now. Games are more immersive and expansive than ever before (though VR still didn't take off). Messaging, both professional and private. If you want to you can let your agent do that for you, and only intervene if you want to - you'll get daily summaries delivered to you in the format you want (remember phrases like "tell me a poem but in the style of Die Hard Bruce Willis"?), and you get to (re)set the agenda for the interactions with each other person's agent. Search has finally become a commodity, and the same goes for research. Multi-agent systems work tirelessly to dig up, compile and summarize and compose content about information.

And make predictions. And that's there the pissing off comes back into the picture. The first big publicly visible shock came in 2031, when North Dakota police - infamously the most militarized police force of any state in the US - or, more precisely, one of their their GRIMs (Guardian Robot for Integrated Monitoring; a robotic, autonomous patrol vehicle that can fire "less-lethal" hard rubber rounds) killed a 14-year old black boy which was playing with a toy tank in the street. In the ensuing court case, the judge ruled that the police wasn't liable, because a machine made the decision autonomously. West Fargo burned on the night of the decision, leading to a curfew imposed on the poorest population of the state. This single incident led to a ripple effect. But let's back up for a second.

All of this is not to say everything is bad in 2035, not at all. As a matter of fact, there is even UBI (universal basic income) available to some people, particularly urban areas of California and Delaware, sponsored by EF, the Equality Fund, which is funded by eight of the ten largest tech companies.

But that's not where the pissing off comes from. Not from the deaths, not from the lost jobs, not from the still ever so slightly uncanny valley effects people get from consuming a lot of your daily content. It comes from living your everyday life. AI has now seeped into every nook and cranny of everyday life, powered by big tech money's ambitions to build AGI (ROI). The real perpetrator wasn't AI, it was marketing. Like any flawed product or business model, and like anything that gets pushed into the market being a beta, and really being an alpha, and really just being a bunch of promises that might or might not be fulfillable (just add water), AI needed to double triple Amazon the crap out of marketing to drive adaptation, and referrals, and retention, and then quintuple Uber the hell out of marketing to keep those numbers up, and the horrible Sword of Damocles that is NPS above 70. Because the quarternary economy needs to be foie-gras pressure fed money to keep pushing towards that ephemeral goal of AGI because it is a loss center.

This meant that promises had to be made and sold, lots of them. And because of the Moby Dick money that was being pumped into these fail whales, and because investors need the money printers to go brrrr, marketed it was.

AI was everywhere. From the partner selector in your dating app to the govtech software that ran your social security payouts. And that's exactly where the problem was. Because it wasn't just bad. It was worse: it was annoying. From getting a parking ticket because the detection algorithm was wrong to your child getting bad grades because AI false positived plagiarization to your mother not getting a doctor's appointment because the algo fucked up the classification of her pain to your recent food poisoning because of an app-wise misclassification of that one (or the other) mushroom you had foraged to your potential (read: not) date telling you that your messages were containing subtle pornographic references to one of your twenty-four supplements causing lactose intolerance but you have no idea which one to you not being able to get a bank account after moving to a new state because some dead person somewhere in the world had really bad credit and a criminal record and lots of similarity to your digital DNA to your fiancée not getting through to the county's clerk office because the phone funnel doesn't believe you to opening up your delivered food just to find an indescribable moosh that looks like a mix of the Gremlins and water, all because AI fucked up. All because AI fucked up. AI fucked up.

AI fucked up.

And that's why people are pissed. The. Fuck. Off.

Because we as a species can take any degree of mockery, tyranny, degradation, denial of rights, as long as you don't break one fundamental law underlying everything. As long as you don't fuck with the one thing that describes our species best. The white balance of our photos. The ketchup on our fries. Convenience.

If you make our everyday lives more tedious, there will be blood in the streets. There will be torching of cars. There will be looting. Or worse: least there will be opt-out. All we need is a trigger to unleash the air from your bubble in one big Jackass-worthy 💨, and all of our built-up resentment and anger about you upon… you. Remember that black boy from WestFargo? His name was Travis. Travis was that trigger.

And so people opted out in masses. In troves. By the hundreds of millions. New product and startup creation couldn't even begin to keep up with the exitus of all exitae.

## Whatever.

People were fucking off. The hashtag #fuckalgos ironically spread on YouTube (which was still alive and kicking, who woulda thunk) like wildfire, trending #1 for eight weeks and amassing seven trillion views in that time, demonstrations in the streets and looting of some of the less well-protected tech company offices ensued. Some tech prominence threw themselves onto the side of the people, only to get shamed back out again. Politics did the usual, being reactive, and promised big changes to AI law, with some governments issuing cease and desist orders (hi EU 👋). Big tech paused lots of its developments, rolled back products and reimbursed subscription fees. All in all, it all looked like a victory by the people for the people. Giddy. Everybody outside of tech was giddy for a moment. They had overcome complacent consumerism and realized something extremely important and acted on it, namely that:

AI doesn't exist. There is no fidelity in AI. AI is dangerously biased.

## A new damn

Giddy was, ironically, the name of the first new manual social media platform, "manual" being the new popular term for anything that is not AI-automated. Unfortunately and unexpectedly, the first and last purpose of this platform was to be forced to witness the downfall of society.

It could have been predicted. Thus it is only unexpected, not unpredictable. The thing with hype trains is, they make it hard for people with contrarian opinions to be heard. There was a man called Andrew Johnson, formerly part of the tech bubble, who had since (we're talking end of the 2020s) started investigating the economic effects of AI and potential scenarios. How nobody else had been able to call out the obvious, he had no clue: AI money had gulped up the global economy, and a collapse of the quartenary sector would let the Big Short look like kids playing with marbles. He went on air, he gave interviews, he vrlogged, he x-ed about total societal chaos… They wouldn't listen to him - partially because similar to Big Tech before Big AI, Big AI had entrenched governments and legislation, and - this time for real - media. And in the end, despite all of the tech platform lock-in, the network effects, the deep everyday slice-of-life integration of "AI", the consumer was still able to opt out. To stop paying. And thus ensued the largest deflation in the history of man…

"What do you mean they're fleeing?"

"Like sheep from a pack of wolves!"

"Like what? What the fuck…"

"Nevermind. This is absolutely critical, I swear, otherwise I wouldn't have called you. Check out the exec dashboard in Looker and tell me I'm lying!"

-silence-

"You have to come down to the office! Now!"

At first he didn't believe his eyes. It looked like a glitch of sorts.

*I must have fucked up my microdose. Also who the fuck does this idiot think he is, calling me at three in the morning…*

It wasn't a glitch. He had not overmicrosdosed his LSD. They were actually fleeing like the proverbial rats the proverbial ship. How? Why? No! No no no, he did not yet want to believe what he saw. He grabbed his laptop, which conveniently sat on his nightstand. Work-life whatever.

"What's going on, honey?"

"Go back to sleep."

It was not a glitch. The line pointed straight down, for about half a centimeter. He changed the resolution. They had lost 555.789 users in the past hour. At three fucking a fucking m. What the actual fuck was going on.

As he heard the helicopter outside, he put his pants on, and then he grabbed a t-shirt - and then dropped it again. Time for a shirt, Frank thought to himself.

"Is everything alright, babe?"

"Just a glitch. Go back to sleep."

She was used to him rushing off to the office like that. He was more work than man.

As he was rushed from the helipad into the building, his Chief of Staff Eric briefed him on the situation:

"We have lost 555.789 users in the past hour or so."

The ground was shifting underneath his feet.

"Thank you very much, Dr. motherfucking Obvious, tell me something I can't see in Looker myself."

"That's it. Geo is global, no other socio-demographic discriminators, we're still trying to find out whether this is external or internal[ly caused]. Oh, and it has stopped."

They entered the boardroom.

"What the fuck do you mean stopped. Somebody put the fucking dashboard on screen and call Suzie. Really glad to be working with professionals!" He hated being woken up at night.

"Why should I trust my eyes on this? Where's the engineer who created this dashboard?"

"Here, sir, Andrew Chen, Sr. looker engineer. We've already checked the data. It's all correct. No idea as to the root cause, sir, but it seems these users simply all terminated their accounts between 02:00 and 03:03 am."

"Is there any more intel on this? Come on, don't make me think!"

"Frank, this is Suzie." Suzie was the only board member he trusted. VC. Earned her stripes in the 2020s crisis. Old guard.

"Suzie, hang on a second. Ladies and gentlemen, you have twenty minutes to come up with an answer to the question WHAT THE EXACT FUCK HAPPENED AND WHY. OUT!"

They had overlooked the obvious. These were all paying users. Now they knew. Did it help? No. Why oh why had it happened at this hour, why only for about one hour what started it and why did it stop? Suzie had promised to keep a lid on it for one hour, but they were still as smart as when they started. Was this a first landslide out of many? Could it happen again? How often? How bad could it become? The stock price will be…

"It's external!" Jerry, Director of Business Intelligence, interrupted Frank's thoughts. "No tech causes. All services up and running!" gasped the Director of SRE, as he returned to the room together with Jerry.

"Twenty-three minutes and this is the best you've got for me?" Counterintuitively, Frank had calmed down substantially.

Andrew slammed the door open. "It's on Giddy!"

"How? Where? Details! Why did our BI trackers not catch any of this?"

"Secret groups, sir." *Stupid privacy fucking up my bonus,* Frank thought.

"Get Steph on the phone!" Steph was the CEO of Giddy. Steph should not give Frank any of the preferential access to data he was going to ask for in a minute. Steph was also pregnant. You have three guesses by whom, if not by her husband. Correct. Silicon Valley is a small, incestuous place, Frank thought, as a cascade of potential headlines erupted in his cerebrum.

*Concentrate, you idiot!*

Frank gulped down a big sip of black coffee. Maybe the root cause wasn't external in the end. Maybe a Netflix Infinite content agent was going rogue in biblical proportions. Maybe this was just the beginning of a much larger scandal...

Maybe they all had no clue what was coming.
